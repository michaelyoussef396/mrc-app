## Part 1 — Start here

### What this app is

This app runs the whole MRC job pipeline, from a customer's first enquiry
through to the final paid invoice. Technicians use it on their phones to see
their jobs, fill in inspection forms, take photos, and record completed
work. The admin uses it on a computer to book inspections and jobs, review
reports before they go to customers, and track invoices and payments. The
app writes the customer-facing reports, sends the customer emails, and
posts updates to the team Slack automatically. Everything you type is saved
to the one shared system, so what a technician records on site is exactly
what the admin sees back at the office.

### Logging in

1. Open the app in your browser (mrcsystem.com) or tap the MRC icon on
   your phone if it's installed.
2. You'll see the sign-in screen headed "MRC internal system" with the line
   "Sign in to your workspace".
3. At the top, tap either "Admin" or "Technician" — pick the one that
   matches your role.
4. Type your email and password in the two boxes.
5. Turn on "Remember me" if it's your own device.
6. Tap "Sign In".

Forgot your password? Tap "Forgot Password?" under the password box, enter
your email, and tap "Send Reset Link". Check your inbox (and spam folder) —
the link expires in 1 hour. If no email arrives after a few minutes, use
"Resend email".

### Installing it on your phone

The app installs like a normal app, straight from the browser — there's
nothing to download from an app store.

On iPhone:

1. Open the app in Safari and log in.
2. Tap the Share button (the square with the arrow).
3. Tap "Add to Home Screen", then "Add".

On Android:

1. Open the app in Chrome and log in.
2. Tap the three-dot menu.
3. Tap "Install app" (or "Add to Home screen").

The icon appears on your home screen as "MRC". Tapping it opens the app
full-screen, starting on the technician home page — it's built for
technicians in the field. Admins working on a computer don't need to
install anything; just use the browser.

### Why the app sometimes updates itself overnight

New versions of the app install themselves automatically in the background.
There's no update button and nothing you need to do — the next time you
open the app after a new version ships, you're already on it. If the app
ever looks half-updated or odd, close it completely and open it again.

### Who to contact for what

- The app is broken (won't load, error messages, something crashed): open
  Settings in the app, then "Help & Support", and use the contact options
  on that screen. That reaches the developer directly.
- A business or pricing question (a rate looks wrong, a rule is unclear,
  "should I be charging for this?"): message Michael.
- A customer complaint or a customer reply to a system email: these land in
  the admin@mouldandrestoration.com.au inbox. Handle them there, or call
  the business line, 1800 954 117. Never give a customer anyone's personal
  mobile — the business line is the number on every email we send.

## Part 2 — For technicians

### Finding today's jobs

Open the app. The home screen greets you by name and shows today's work:

- The "Next Up" card is your next job — customer name, time, address, and
  three buttons: "Start Inspection", "Get Directions", and "View Lead".
  "Get Directions" shows the estimated travel time when the app knows it.
- Below that, "Today's Jobs" lists the rest of the day.
- If an admin has sent work back to you, a "Revisions Needed" banner
  appears at the top with the admin's note and a "Revise Job Completion"
  button. Do these first.

For anything beyond today, tap "My Jobs" in the bottom bar. Filter pills
across the top: "Today", "This Week", "This Month", "Overdue",
"Pending Review", "All". Each job card has "Call", "Directions", a main
action button ("Start Inspection", "Resume Inspection",
"Start Job Completion" or "Continue Job Completion"), and "View Lead".

Tapping "View Lead" opens the full job details: site prep notes, contact
info, the address with "View on Google Maps", access instructions, and
internal notes. The big button at the bottom is always your next step.

### Doing an inspection, section by section

Tap "Start Inspection" on the job. The form has 9 sections — the header
shows which one you're on ("Section 3 of 9") and a percentage.

1. "Basic Information" — the job and customer basics.
2. "Property Details" — dwelling type and property information.
3. "Area Inspection" — one entry per affected area: readings, findings,
   and photos for each room.
4. "Subfloor" — whether the property has a subfloor and its readings. You
   must answer this one way or the other before you can finish.
5. "Outdoor Info" — outdoor conditions and comments.
6. "Waste Disposal" — see "Equipment and waste on site" below.
7. "Work Procedure" — the treatment methods you're quoting, the option
   pricing ("Option 1 / Surface Treatment", "Both / Show Both Prices",
   "Option 2 / Comprehensive"), and the equipment quantities.
8. "Job Summary" — hours and summary details.
9. "Cost Estimate" — the calculated quote.

Moving around:

- "Next Section" and "Previous" move between sections and save as they go.
- The "Save" button saves any time. The form also auto-saves every 30
  seconds — you'll see a small "Auto-saved" message.
- On the last section the button reads "Complete". Tapping it checks
  everything, and if something's missing you'll see "Missing Required
  Fields" with the list. Fix them and tap "Complete" again.
- While the report content is being written you'll see "Generating AI
  Summary". Wait for it. When it finishes you'll see "Inspection
  Complete" and be taken back to your home screen. The admin takes it
  from there.

If you try to leave with unsaved changes, the app asks before letting you
go.

### Taking photos and adding captions

Every photo needs a caption — the app asks for the caption first, before
you pick the photo.

1. Tap the add-photo button in the section you're in.
2. A box appears (for example "Add Room Photo") with a "Caption" field.
   Describe what the photo shows, like the example text suggests — e.g.
   "Visible mould on ceiling above shower".
3. Tap "Choose Photo" and take or pick the photo. The button stays greyed
   out until you've typed a caption.

Rules to know:

- Captions can be up to 500 characters.
- Each photo spot takes up to 4 photos — you'll see "Maximum 4 photos" if
  you hit the limit.
- You must save the inspection once before photos can upload — if you see
  "Save First", tap "Save" and try again.
- If a photo ever fails to sync because its caption went missing, a red
  bar appears at the top: "1 photo couldn't sync — review required". Tap
  "Review", then either "Add caption & retry" or "Discard" for each photo.

### What happens if you lose signal

Be straight about this: the form does not work offline. It needs a
connection to save to the system. Here is what actually happens without
signal:

- An amber bar appears: "You're offline — new changes stay on this device
  until you're back online."
- If a save fails, you'll see "You're offline — not saved to the server.
  Your changes are only on this device for now. Keep this form open — it
  will save to the server automatically once you're back online." Do
  exactly that: keep the form open. Don't close the app.
- If you tap "Complete" while offline, nothing is sent. You'll see
  "You're offline — inspection not submitted". Your answers stay on the
  device — keep the form open and tap "Complete" again when signal
  returns.
- Photos are the exception: photos cannot be added offline and are NOT
  kept on the device. You'll see a message telling you to add the photo
  again once you're back online. Remember which photos you still owe.

There is also a safety net: the form keeps a backup on your device,
refreshed every 30 seconds. If the app crashes or the page reloads, reopen
the form within 24 hours and you'll see "Unsaved work found" with a
"Restore" button — tap it and your work comes back.

One honest gap: a brand-new inspection that has never saved even once has
no backup yet. If the device dies before that very first save, that first
sitting is lost. So tap "Save" early on every new inspection.

### Completing a job, section by section

When the remediation work is done, open the job and tap "Start Job
Completion". The form has 10 sections; the header shows "Section N of 10".

1. "Office Info" — job number, address, and who requested the work are
   filled in for you. You can set "Attention To" (e.g. a property
   manager).
2. "Summary" — turn on "SWMS Completed" (you can't submit without it),
   pick the "Premises Type" (Residential or Commercial), set the
   "Completion Date", and check the "Areas Treated" list. Areas come
   across from the inspection; tap the × to remove one or "Add Area" for
   a new one.
3. "Before Photos" — pick up to 10 photos from the inspection to include
   in the job report. Tap photos to select them; the counter shows how
   many you've picked.
4. "After Photos" — photograph each area after remediation. The number of
   after photos must match the number of before photos you selected. If
   demolition was done, turn on "Demolition Works" — that opens a
   demolition photo section needing exactly 4 photos, plus a
   "Justification" and "Removal Notes".
5. "Treatment Methods" — switch on each method you actually used. There
   are 11: HEPA Vacuuming, Surface Mould Remediation, ULV Fogging
   Property, ULV Fogging Subfloor, Subfloor Remediation, HEPA Air
   Scrubber Installation, Drying Equipment, Containment & PRV, Material
   Demolition, Cavity Treatment, Debris Removal. At least one is
   required.
6. "Chemical Toggles" — switch on what was applied: Air Filtration
   Treatment, Water Based Solution, Sodium Hypochlorite, HEPA Vac'd,
   Sanitised Premises.
7. "Equipment Used" — see the next heading.
8. "Variations" — if the work changed from the original quote, turn on
   "Scope Changed" and fill in "What Changed", "Why Changed", "Extra Work
   Performed", and "Scope Reduced" as they apply. What-and-why are
   required when the toggle is on.
9. "Job Notes" — "Request Admin Review" (see below), "Damages Present"
   and "Staining Present" toggles with detail boxes, and "Additional
   Notes".
10. "Office Notes" — you'll see "Office notes are only visible to admin
    users." This section is for the office, not you.

On the last section the button reads "Complete". If anything's missing
you'll see "Cannot submit — please fix the following:" with the reasons,
and the form jumps you to the first thing to fix.

This form auto-saves every 30 seconds too ("Saved to the server"), keeps
the same on-device backup with the same "Unsaved work found" / "Restore"
offer, and has the same offline behaviour: keep it open until it saves.

### Equipment and waste on site

In "Equipment Used" there's a card for each item — dehumidifier, air
mover, HEPA air scrubber and RCD box — showing its daily rate.

Set "Qty" and "Days" for what you actually used. Under each card you'll
see what was quoted at inspection ("Quoted: 2 units for 3 days"). If your
actual quantity is higher than quoted, the card turns amber and says
"Actual quantity exceeds quoted amount" — that's not an error, just a flag
the admin will see. The "Total Equipment Cost" at the bottom excludes GST.

The "Waste Disposal" card works on volume: enter the "Actual volume
removed (m³)". The app prices it and shows "Price unconfirmed" until you
tap the "Confirm Price" button, which shows the amount. If the real cost
was different, tap "Override", type the price, and tap "Save Override".
Changing the volume clears the confirmation, so confirm again after any
edit. You cannot submit while a waste price sits unconfirmed.

The same idea exists on the inspection form's "Waste Disposal" section
when quoting: turn on "Waste Disposal Required", set the "Bin size (m³)",
and tap "Confirm Price" (or override it) before finishing the inspection.

For rates and pricing rules, see the pricing guide.

### Asking for a review of your work, and what happens next

In "Job Notes", turn on "Request Admin Review" if you want the office to
look over the job before the customer report is generated — use it when
something unusual happened: damages, a big variation, anything you'd
rather a second set of eyes saw. The helper text confirms: "This job will
be flagged for admin review before the report is generated."

What happens after you tap "Complete":

- With review requested: you'll see "Job flagged for admin review". The
  admin sees your submission and either taps "Approve & Generate Report"
  (job proceeds, report is generated) or "Send Back to Technician" with a
  note.
- Without: you'll see "Job completion submitted successfully" and the job
  report is generated for the admin to review and send.
- If it comes back to you, the job shows the "Admin requested changes"
  banner with the note. Open it with "Revise Job Completion", fix what's
  asked, and tap "Complete" again.

### Setting your starting address

Your starting address is where the app measures travel time from for your
first job each day. Set it once, and update it if you move.

1. Tap "Profile" in the bottom bar, then "My Profile".
2. Tap "Edit Profile".
3. In "Starting Address", start typing your home address.
4. This is the important part: pick your address from the dropdown list
   that appears. Don't just type it and save — typed text that wasn't
   picked from the list silently doesn't save, and the old address stays.
5. Tap "Save Changes".

You can also change your name, phone number, and profile photo here, and
"Change Password" sends you a reset link. Your email can't be changed here
— ask the admin.

## Part 3 — For admin

### Reading the dashboard

The dashboard is your morning screen. From top to bottom:

- "Needs Attention" — leads waiting on you, shown first for a reason.
- The stat cards: "Today's Jobs", "Leads to Assign", "Completed This
  Week", "Revenue This Week", "Pending Reviews" (tap it to jump straight
  to those leads), "Overdue Invoices" (count and dollar total — tap to
  jump), and "Failed Webhooks" (only appears if a website enquiry failed
  to arrive; if you ever see it, tell the developer).
- "Today's Schedule" — every booking today with time, client, technician,
  type and a coloured stage pill. "View All" opens the Schedule.
- "Outstanding Invoices" — every unpaid invoice, with "Due" dates and how
  many days overdue.
- "Unassigned Leads" — new leads with no technician yet, and a "Schedule
  Inspections" button.
- "Team Workload" — how many leads each technician is carrying.
- "Quick Actions" — "New Lead", "Approve Reports", "Calendar", "Reports".

"Revenue This Week" counts invoices actually paid this week — not quotes,
not sent invoices.

### Moving a lead through the pipeline

Every lead sits at exactly one stage. The full journey, in order, with
what each stage means:

1. "New Lead" — just arrived; book the inspection.
2. "Awaiting Inspection" — booked; technician to inspect.
3. "AI Summary Review" — inspection done; review the report wording.
4. "Approve Inspection Report" — check the PDF itself.
5. "Email Approval" — send the report to the customer.
6. "Awaiting Job" — customer approved; book the remediation job.
7. "Job Scheduled" — job booked; technician to do the work.
8. "Job Completed" — work done; review and send the job report.
9. "Pending Review" — the technician asked you to review the job first.
10. "Job Report Sent" — job report emailed; invoice next.
11. "Invoice Sent" — invoice logged as sent; awaiting payment.
12. "Paid" — payment received; send the review request.
13. "Google Review" — review request sent; close out when done.
14. "Finished" — all done.
15. "Closed" — completed and closed (older path).
16. "Not Landed" — lost or rejected, with the reason recorded.

You rarely change stages by hand. On the lead page the big button always
offers the correct next step for the current stage — "Schedule
Inspection", "Review AI Summary", "Book Remediation Job", "Approve &
Generate Report", and so on. Work through those buttons and the lead
moves itself.

To force a stage change anyway, open the lead and tap "Status" (on a
phone, the three-dot menu then "Change Status"). Pick the new stage from
the list — the current one is marked "Current". Use this sparingly:
moving a lead backwards can clear booking and payment details.

The "Leads" page lists everything, with pipeline filter pills across the
top for each stage, a search box ("Search by name, address, email, or
phone..."), sorting, and card/list views. "New Lead" (top right) opens
the manual lead form — name, preferred date and time, address (pick from
the suggestions), email, description, and lead source. New leads are
automatically posted to the team Slack.

To drop a lead, use "Not Proceeding" (on the lead's action button at the
right stage) or the remove option — you'll pick a reason ("Too
Expensive", "Went with Competitor", and so on) and the lead moves to "Not
Landed". It can be reactivated later. "Archive Lead" hides a lead without
recording a loss reason.

### Booking an inspection or job

Inspections are booked from the Schedule page. Leads that need booking
queue up on the right under "To Schedule".

1. Open "Schedule" and tap the lead in the "To Schedule" queue.
2. Step 1: confirm the address. Tap "Address is Correct", or "Update
   Address" and pick the right one from the search suggestions, then
   "Confirm Address". Scheduling stays locked until this is done.
3. Set "Est. Duration (minutes)" — from 30 minutes up to 8 hours, in
   15-minute steps. It starts at 60.
4. Pick the technician under "Assign Technician".
5. Check "Recommended Days" — the app suggests the best days for that
   technician, with their travel time from home.
6. Set the "Inspection Date" and pick a "Time Slot" — slots run on the
   hour, 7:00 AM to 7:00 PM.
7. Read the travel check. If the technician can't make it from their
   previous job you'll see a red warning ("Tech needs 25 more min to get
   there..."). You can still book, but you've been told.
8. Tap "Book Inspection".

The app blocks double-bookings: if the technician already has something
at that time you'll see "Technician already booked at this time" and
which booking clashes. Pick another slot or technician.

On booking, the customer automatically gets an email ("Inspection Booking
Confirmed") and the team gets a Slack post. If the lead has no email
address, the app warns you ("No email on file") and asks you to confirm —
the booking still works, the customer just gets no email. Two days before
the inspection the customer also gets an automatic reminder email.

Jobs are booked from the lead page: at "Awaiting Job", tap "Book
Remediation Job". The "Book Job" panel shows the quote summary, then:
pick the "Technician", "Start Date", and "Start Time" (operating hours
7:00 AM – 7:00 PM), and check the hours — the app splits the job into
consecutive days and shows the "Day Schedule". A clash shows "Booking
conflict detected" and blocks the booking. Tap "Confirm Booking — 3
day(s)" (or whatever it says). The customer gets a "Job Booking
Confirmed" email. Rebooking later uses the same panel via "Reschedule".

### Reviewing the AI summary and sending the report

When a technician completes an inspection, the lead lands at "AI Summary
Review". Open the lead and tap "Review AI Summary".

The page shows the lead context on the left and four report sections you
can edit directly: "What We Found", "Problem Analysis &
Recommendations", "What We're Going To Do", and "Demolition Details"
(only when demolition is involved).

- Edit the text directly, or have the AI rewrite it: type an instruction
  in "Tell the AI what to change" and tap "Regenerate All Sections", or
  use the "Regenerate" button inside one section for just that section.
  After regenerating, remember to tap "Save Draft" — regenerated text
  isn't saved until you do.
- Heed the "Verify before approving" warning if it appears: it means the
  report mentions a service the inspection didn't record. An unselected
  service in the report is a commitment to unquoted work — fix the
  wording or confirm it's really intended.
- "Reject" sends the lead back for re-inspection. "Approve & Next" moves
  the lead on to the report itself.

Next stage is the report PDF. Open it via "View & Edit Report":

- "Edit Mode" lets you adjust fields; "Regenerate" refreshes the preview
  after edits.
- "Download" is more than a download — it saves a numbered version to the
  history ("Downloaded v3 — saved to history"). It takes about 10
  seconds. The email attachment always comes from a saved version, so
  you must Download at least once before sending.
- "Approve & Send" (then "Send Email to Customer" on the email screen)
  sends the report. The "To", "Subject" and "Message" are pre-filled,
  and you can change any of them before sending.
- "History" shows every saved version, when it was made, and whether it
  was emailed.

Two guards protect you at send time. If the report data changed since the
version you saved, a box appears: "Report data has changed since v3" —
choose "Send v3 as-is" or "Hard-save fresh & send" (almost always pick
fresh). If this report was already emailed, you'll see "Report already
emailed" with the date — only tap "Send Again" if you truly want a
duplicate in the customer's inbox.

After the inspection report is sent, the lead moves to "Awaiting Job".
The job report at "Job Completed" works the same way — review, Download,
"Review & Send Job Report".

### What the stale-PDF warning means and when to regenerate

If you see the yellow banner "PDF is out of date. Regenerate before
sending to customer.", it means the report content has been edited (or
the AI summary re-saved) since the PDF was last generated — the PDF on
screen no longer matches the latest data. Tap "Regenerate PDF", check the
result, then Download and send. Never send past this banner without
regenerating; the customer would get the old version.

### Creating an invoice

First, understand the split: the real invoice is created in Xero. The
app's invoice screen calculates the correct figures for you to copy into
Xero, and then tracks the invoice through sent → paid. The app never
emails invoices to customers.

From the lead (at "Job Completed" or later), open the invoice screen via
"Open full invoice editor" (or the invoice summary card). It pre-fills:

- Labour hours from the inspection ("Non-demolition hours", "Demolition
  hours", "Subfloor hours"). The labour cost is worked out for you.
- "Equipment used (from job completion)" — each item with Qty, Days and
  line total.
- Waste disposal.

Where the inspection estimate and the job actuals differ, you'll see
"Inspection estimate and job actual differ — choose one or type below"
with two small chips — "Estimate: ..." and "Actual: ..." — each with a
"Use" button. Tap the one to bill. Nothing is chosen for you.

Add anything extra under "Custom Line Items" ("Add item") — variations
and miscellaneous charges go here.

The totals build up on screen: labour, "Equipment subtotal (never
discounted)", "Waste disposal (ex GST, never discounted)", custom items,
"Subtotal ex GST", "GST 10%", "Total inc GST".

"Payment Terms" offers 7, 14, 30 or 60 days. One thing to know: when you
later mark the invoice sent, the due date restarts at 14 days from the
send date regardless — terms start when the invoice actually goes out.

"Save Draft" keeps your work without advancing anything. Invoice numbers
look like INV-2026-0005 and are issued automatically.

There is no discount box on this screen. For rates, discounts and pricing
rules, see the pricing guide.

### Marking sent and marking paid

Once you've raised the invoice in Xero and sent it:

1. On the invoice screen, tap "Mark Invoice as Sent to Client".
2. The confirmation asks: "Have you created this invoice in Xero and are
   ready to mark it as sent?" Tap "Yes, mark as sent".
3. The lead advances to "Invoice Sent", the due date is set to 14 days
   from today, and the team is notified on Slack.

When the money arrives:

1. Tap "Mark as Paid" (on the invoice screen) or "Mark Paid" (on the
   lead's "Invoice & Payment" card).
2. Pick the payment method — Bank Transfer, Cash, Visa, Mastercard, or
   Cheque — set the payment date, and add a reference if you have one
   (bank ref, receipt number).
3. Tap "Confirm paid". The lead advances to "Paid" and Slack is notified.

The lead page's "Invoice & Payment" card shows the amount, number, due
date and days overdue at a glance, with "Edit", "Void Invoice" (kept in
history, can't be reactivated), and the note that marking sent "Logs the
invoice for tracking; does not email the customer."

If you type a total that differs from the calculated figure, the app asks
you to confirm a manual override and records that it was one.

### Overdue invoices and what the Slack message means

Every morning at 9:00 AM the system checks all sent invoices. If nothing
newly crossed a line, it stays silent. When something did, one combined
Slack digest is posted (never one message per invoice). It can contain:

- "Newly overdue (flagged this run)" — invoices that just passed their
  due date, with amounts and days overdue.
- "Penalty milestones reached today" — invoices crossing a stage of the
  penalty ladder. The stages, by days past due: day 1 "Overdue" (warranty
  suspended), day 8 "Second Reminder", day 15 "Final Notice", day 16
  "Warranty VOID", day 29 "Warranty VOID — Ongoing". The fees and
  interest attached to each stage are in the pricing guide.
- "Admin escalation" at day 60 — stop chasing, escalate. That's a prompt
  to you, not another penalty stage.
- The outstanding total across all unpaid invoices.

Two things the system does NOT do: it never contacts the customer (the
digest itself says so — following up is yours to do from the dashboard),
and it never charges the fees or interest. Those figures are
informational per our terms; Xero calculates and collects them, not this
app.

### Sending the review request, and when NOT to

When a lead reaches "Paid", a "Request Google Review" card appears on the
lead page: "Payment received. Send a branded email asking the customer to
leave a Google review." Tap "Send Google Review Request". The customer
gets a friendly branded email with a "Leave us a Google Review" button,
and the lead moves to "Google Review". When you're done with the lead,
tap "Close Lead" — the confirmation warns "This cannot be undone" — and
it's "Finished".

When NOT to send it:

- The customer complained, disputed the invoice, or the job had problems.
  A review request lands badly there — resolve first, then decide.
- The customer previously replied STOP. The review email offers an
  opt-out ("reply STOP to this email"), but nothing in the system reads
  those replies automatically. If a STOP reply arrives in the admin
  inbox, honour it by hand: note it and never send that customer another
  request. An opt-out we offered and ignored is worse than no opt-out.

## Part 4 — When something looks wrong

Can't log in

- What it means: wrong details, wrong portal, or no role on the account.
- What to do: check you picked the right "Admin" / "Technician" toggle on
  the sign-in screen. Try "Forgot Password?" and reset. If you see "No
  Permissions Assigned", your account exists but has no role.
- Who to call: for "No Permissions Assigned" or a locked account, the
  admin (Michael). For a broken sign-in screen, Help & Support.

Photo won't upload

- What it means: usually no caption, no signal, a hit limit, or the
  inspection hasn't been saved yet.
- What to do: read the message. "Save First" — tap "Save", retry.
  "Maximum 4 photos" — that spot is full. Offline warning — photos are
  not kept offline; add the photo again when signal returns. A red
  "couldn't sync — review required" bar — tap "Review", then "Add caption
  & retry".
- Who to call: if photos fail repeatedly with good signal, Help & Support.

Form won't save

- What it means: no connection, or the form is blocking a save it knows
  would be wrong (e.g. a two-option quote with hours missing).
- What to do: if the message mentions being offline, keep the form open —
  it saves itself once you're back online. If it says an option total
  "could not be computed", enter the missing hours it names. Otherwise
  tap "Save" again.
- Who to call: repeated "Could not save" with good signal → Help &
  Support.

Report didn't send

- What it means: one of the send guards stopped it, or the lead has no
  email address.
- What to do: if you see "Please click Download first to save a reviewed
  version, then Send" — do exactly that. If the "Report data has
  changed" box appears, pick "Hard-save fresh & send". If "Report
  already emailed" appears, it DID send before — only "Send Again" for a
  deliberate resend. Check the customer's email address on the lead.
- Who to call: if sending fails after all that, Help & Support.

Wrong price showing

- What it means: either an input is off (quantities, days, hours,
  unconfirmed waste price) or a rate is genuinely wrong.
- What to do: check the inputs first — equipment Qty and Days, labour
  hours, and whether the waste price shows "Price unconfirmed". Check the
  rate itself against the pricing guide.
- Who to call: if a rate itself is wrong, message Michael immediately —
  the same rate feeds every quote and invoice.

App looks out of date

- What it means: an update shipped and your device is showing a stale
  copy.
- What to do: close the app fully and reopen it. On a computer, refresh
  the page. Updates install themselves — no store visit needed.
- Who to call: if it still looks wrong after a full close, Help & Support.

Customer says they got no email

- What it means: no email on their lead, a typo in it, or it's in spam.
- What to do: open the lead, check the email address. The activity log on
  the lead shows what was sent and when. Ask them to check spam — our
  emails come from admin@mouldandrestoration.com.au.
- Who to call: if the address is right and emails repeatedly don't
  arrive, Help & Support.

## Part 6 — Word list

Plain meanings for words you'll see on screen.

- HEPA — a very fine air filter grade; "HEPA Vacuuming" is vacuuming
  with that filter.
- HEPA Air Scrubber — the air-cleaning unit we hire out by the unit per
  day; the older name "AFD" means the same machine.
- AFD — old name for the HEPA Air Scrubber.
- Subfloor — the space under the floor of the house; some jobs need it
  inspected and treated separately.
- SWMS — Safe Work Method Statement, the safety paperwork; the job form
  can't be submitted until it's marked completed.
- RCD — the safety switch box hired alongside electrical drying gear,
  charged by the day.
- ULV Fogging — treating a space with a fine chemical mist.
- Containment & PRV — sealing off a work area and managing its air
  pressure so spores don't spread.
- Dehumidifier — dries the air, hired by the day. Air Mover — an
  industrial fan, also hired by the day.
- m³ — cubic metres, how waste volume is measured and priced.
- GST — the 10% tax added last on every quote and invoice.
- Lead — one customer enquiry and everything that happens to it,
  from first call to final payment.
- Pipeline — the ordered stages every lead moves through.
- Pending Review — a job the technician has flagged for the admin to
  check before the report is generated.
- Revision — a job the admin sent back to the technician with a note on
  what to fix.
- Variation / Scope Changed — the work done differed from the original
  quote; the what-and-why get recorded and flow to the invoice.
- Hard save — tapping "Download" on a report, which stores a numbered
  version in the history; only saved versions can be emailed.
- Version history — the list of every saved copy of a report, with dates
  and whether each was emailed.
- Stale PDF — the yellow warning that the report data changed after the
  PDF was made; regenerate before sending.
- Quarantined photo — a photo held back from syncing because it lost its
  caption; add the caption and retry, or discard it.
- Penalty ladder — the escalating late-payment stages an unpaid invoice
  climbs (day 1, 8, 15, 16, 29); shown for information, charged through
  Xero, not by this app.
- Xero — the accounting system where real invoices are created and
  where late fees and interest are actually calculated.
- Slack — the team chat where the app posts new leads, payments and the
  overdue digest.
- Estimate vs Actual — the inspection's quoted figures versus what the
  job really used; at invoice time you pick which one to bill with the
  "Use" buttons.

The pipeline stages, in one line each:

- New Lead — enquiry arrived; book the inspection.
- Awaiting Inspection — inspection booked, not yet done.
- AI Summary Review — admin is checking the report wording.
- Approve Inspection Report — admin is checking the report PDF.
- Email Approval — report ready to email to the customer.
- Awaiting Job — customer approved; job needs booking.
- Job Scheduled — remediation booked with a technician.
- Job Completed — work done; job report to review and send.
- Pending Review — technician asked admin to review the job first.
- Job Report Sent — job report emailed; invoice next.

- Google Review — review request emailed; close out when done.
- Finished — lead fully complete.
- Closed — completed and closed (an older ending, same idea).
